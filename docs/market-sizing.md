# Target audience size: AI trainers in Brazil

**For:** Xinning. **Researched:** 27 Oct 2026. **Confidence:** low to medium. There is no official count, so every number below is a triangulated estimate with a range.

## Bottom line

| Segment | Estimate (range) | Central |
|---|---|---|
| **Brazilians who work or recently worked on AI-training platforms** (any platform, incl. search/ad raters) | 60,000 – 150,000 | **~90,000** |
| **Active in a given month** | 25,000 – 60,000 | **~40,000** |
| **Core fit for Ganhômetro (SAM):** monthly active on LLM-training platforms with hourly pay (Outlier, DataAnnotation, Mercor, Alignerr, Turing…), about 70% of the monthly active | 18,000 – 42,000 | **~28,000** |
| ...of which earn **more than R$ 5.000/month** (where the carnê-leão matters) | 2,500 – 12,000 | **~6,000** |
| **Realistic year-1 paying users (SOM)** | 50 – 500 | **~180** (≈ R$ 2.700/month) |
| **Long-run ceiling, AI trainers only** (5% of SAM paying) | 900 – 2,100 | ≈ R$ 13–31k/month |
| **Expansion:** Brazilians paid in dollars as freelancers | several hundred thousand (low confidence) | — |

**In one sentence:** the core market is a niche of a few tens of thousands of active people. That's enough for a profitable small product at R$ 14,90, but the expansion to all Brazilians paid in dollars is what makes it a bigger business.

**Caveats:**
- **Self-reported:** company figures (900k, 1M) are marketing claims that count everyone who ever signed up.
- **Ambiguous traffic window:** it's unclear whether Similarweb's 8.3M is one month or three months, so both readings are carried as a range.
- **Traffic share isn't worker share:** applicants also visit, which is why the logged-in app (6.45%) is weighted more than the public site (4.0%).

Many people work on 2–3 platforms. Dividing by an overlap factor of 1.5–2.0 gives **55k–146k unique people**, rounded to **60k–150k (central ~90k)**.

## 3. Method B: activity (people working this month)

- **Outlier:** 8.3M app visits × 6.45% Brazil = **~535k visits/month** (or ~180k if the figure covers 3 months). Assuming an active worker opens the app about 20 times a month, that's **9k–27k active Brazilians**.
- **DataAnnotation:** 390k–860k Brazilian visits a month. If about half are workers, at 20 visits each, that's **10k–21k**.
- **Others:** Appen/TELUS 10k–20k active, Mercor about 1k, other platforms 5k–10k.
- **Total:** 35k–80k. Dividing by an overlap factor of 1.3–1.5 gives 23k–62k, rounded to **25k–60k unique monthly active (central ~40k)**.
- **Core fit (SAM):** about 70% of them work on LLM-training platforms with hourly pay, where tracking unpaid time matters. The rest are mostly search/ad raters. That gives **18k–42k (central ~28k)**.

**Year-1 paying users (SOM):**
- **Signups:** Ganhômetro reaches 5–15% of the core as signups, so about 900 to 6,300 (central ~2,800).
- **Conversion:** 5–8% of signups pay.
- **Result:** about **50–500 paying users (central ~180)**.

Methods A and B agree: roughly **40–45% of people who've worked in the past are active in a given month**, which is typical for on-demand work.

## 4. Who they are and what they earn

- **Profile:** mostly women, university-educated, aged 18–35, in São Paulo, Rio and Minas Gerais. One in three depends entirely on this income (DiPLab).
- **Pay:** Portuguese language work up to US$ 10/h; coding and STEM work US$ 13–27/h; Mercor experts much higher. The 2023 average across all microwork was R$ 9/h.
- **Monthly income** (estimate, at R$ 5,10 per dollar):
  - Casual (5–10 h/week at US$ 10): **R$ 1.100–2.200**
  - Committed (20 h/week at US$ 10–15): **R$ 4.400–6.600**
  - Expert/coder (20 h/week at US$ 25): **~R$ 11.000**
- **Share above R$ 5.000/month:** about **10–20%** of active workers. This confirms the handoff's choice to make the carnê-leão a bonus, not the headline. The R$ 5 mil alert matters to a minority, but a valuable one.
- **Volatility:** app.outlier.ai traffic fell 14% in one month (Sep 2026), and projects and rates change without notice. The market can shrink quickly. That's also the pain Ganhômetro addresses.

## 5. What this means for the plan

1. **300–500 signups by week 8** is about **1–2% of the monthly-active core.** That's realistic if the marketer reaches the right groups.
2. **50 founding sales from 300–500 signups** needs a **10–17% conversion**, which is ambitious for a pre-launch offer (5–10% is more typical for a warm list). **Recommendation:** aim for 800–1,000 signups, or expect the "20–49" outcome and a second sale, as the handoff's gate already allows.
3. **150 paying users** (the break-even point for R$ 2.000/month of marketing) is close to the central year-1 estimate (~180). It's reachable, but with little margin.
4. **Price:** R$ 14,90 is about 0,3–0,7% of a R$ 2.000–5.000 monthly income. That's reasonable for committed workers. Casual workers earning about R$ 9/h are price-sensitive, so the **free plan is essential** to reach them.
5. **Growth needs the expansion.** With AI trainers alone, the ceiling is about R$ 13–31k/month. Opening to all Brazilians paid in dollars (devs, designers, translators) multiplies the market several times over.
6. **Marketing focus:** Instagram and TikTok content aimed at women aged 18–35, Portuguese-language WhatsApp and Telegram groups, and São Paulo, Rio and Minas Gerais.
